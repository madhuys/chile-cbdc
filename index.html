Policy brief · Banco Central de Chile · Nov 2026

The digital peso, not yet.

Chile's central bank has studied new forms of digital money since 2019 1and has tested a CBDC in a sandbox since June 2025 1. Here is what it found, and the four roads now open.

No decision.

The Banco Central de Chile (BCCh) says it still lacks enough information for a definitive decision on issuing a central bank digital currency (CBDC) 1, and that studying it further is not a decision to issue one 1.

The road so far

From blockchain curiosity to a sandbox

The Bank ties its interest to how far payments have gone digital, with new technologies and new players changing how people pay 1. It also notes that, although many central banks are exploring CBDCs, few have decided to issue one 1.

  1. 2019

    Study begins

    The BCCh starts studying new forms of digital money and assets 1.

  2. 2021

    Focus turns to CBDC

    From instruments on programmable blockchains to the benefits and risks of a CBDC 1.

  3. 2022

    First report

    According to Central Banking, the Bank said the payment system "works adequately" 5; wholesale raises fewer problems than retail, and the Bank might issue neither 5.

  4. 2024

    Second report

    According to Blockchain.cl, it announced proofs of concept and set out a two-tier model: the BCCh issues, intermediaries serve the public 6.

  5. 2025

    Proof of concept

    The PoC begins in June 1; it tests a wholesale CBDC only 2.

  6. 2026–27

    Stablecoin rules

    Framework to consultation in late 2026, a rule in 2027 3.

CoinGeek reported that the 2024 report drew on 450 consultation responses and eight working groups 4. Participants named remittances, programmable payments, digital public money and digital settlement as reasons to issue 4.

The payments backdrop

Cash has more than halved

Withdrawals from automated teller machines (ATMs), a rough measure of cash demand for transactions, fell from 14.6% of GDP in 2019 to 6.1% in 2025 3. Each person over 15 now makes 435 digital payments a year on average, up 15% on the previous report 3.

Inside the proof of concept

One ledger. Two legs. Settled at once.

The Bank issued a simulated wholesale CBDC through a smart contract, credited it to fictitious participants as if it were reserves, transferred, redeemed and used it to settle trades 2. It tokenised simulated BCCh notes, inflation-indexed bonds and bank bonds 2.

1Strength

Operational integration

Holding CBDC and assets on one platform simplifies operations, cuts reliance on several infrastructures and enables atomic settlement 2.

2Strength

Programmability

New instruments and business logic can use the same CBDC without redesigning the infrastructure 2.

3Trade-off

Privacy

Ledger transparency conflicts with market confidentiality; the fixes tested add complexity and may limit scalability 2.

4Trade-off

Governance

With the BCCh running every node, control is easier, but functions now spread across institutions become concentrated, which may reduce resilience 2.

The Bank says the PoC does not mean it has decided to issue, and does not prejudge any policy decision 2. Abroad, the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) lists the Central Bank of Chile as an observer of Project mBridge 7.

Whichever road

What has to be in place

01 · SEQUENCING

Stage two of three

Blockchain.cl reports that the plan of research, PoC, then pilot is still at its second stage 6. The stablecoin framework goes to consultation in late 2026 and becomes a rule in 2027 3.

02 · LEGAL BASIS

A framework still to come

The outcome depends on PoC results, industry dialogue and a legal framework that does not yet exist 6. The Fintech Law already lets the BCCh regulate fiat-backed stablecoins for payments 3.

03 · WHO DECIDES

The BCCh, with the IMF's help

The BCCh says it lacks enough information for a definitive decision on issuing a CBDC 1, and that its PoC does not anticipate any policy decision on a wholesale CBDC 2. The stablecoin framework is being drafted with International Monetary Fund (IMF) technical assistance, alongside the rules of the CMF, the financial market regulator 3.

04 · INFRASTRUCTURE

A wider settlement system

Final rules opening the real-time gross settlement (LBTR) system to non-banks, including the securities depository and prepaid issuers, are due shortly 3.

How this was made

This is a policy brief: it sets out options and does not recommend one. The main sources are the BCCh's own CBDC and PoC pages, the FinteChile summary of the BCCh's 2026 Payment Systems Report (ISiP), trade press on the 2022 and 2024 reports, and the BIS mBridge page.

This brief's research could not retrieve the full 2024 report or the BCCh's Spanish-language pages (blocked to automated reading), so some details of that report come from secondary coverage, named in the text (Central Banking, CoinGeek, Blockchain.cl). Cost cells read "not stated" because none of the sources read gives a figure. The only BCCh test found was the wholesale PoC 2, and Blockchain.cl describes the digital peso as a serious exploration with no date set 6.
